# BMC Outreach Playbook

## Objective
Build supply and demand in parallel without requiring cold calls from Brett.

## Campaign A — Vendor recruitment
Target: local service providers that can fulfill BMC-generated leads.

### Sequence
1. Intro email
   - personalized to market + service
   - explain BMC is building a local lead/referral channel
   - invite provider to complete the partner intake
   - no fake claim that a lead is currently waiting
2. Follow-up 1 (3–4 business days)
   - short reminder
   - emphasize territory/service fit and no sales meeting required
3. Follow-up 2 (7–10 business days)
   - offer pilot placement / first qualified lead test where appropriate
4. Close loop
   - mark no-response and suppress for 60–90 days

### Partner CTA
/portfolio/vendor-partner/

## Campaign B — B2B buyer acquisition
Target: restaurants, property managers, HOAs, STR managers, fleets, offices, retail, hospitality, and other buyers of recurring/urgent local services.

### Sequence
1. Identify a concrete service fit.
2. Send a short plain-text email offering the request/quote route.
3. Link directly to the service intake page for the relevant market/service.
4. Follow up once or twice, then suppress.

## Campaign C — Existing-lead vendor routing
Only after a real customer request exists.

1. Match exact market + service.
2. Send qualified job summary to active vendors.
3. Provider accepts/declines.
4. Route to next eligible vendor if declined/expired.
5. Release customer details according to the approved lead model.
6. Track accepted → contacted → estimate → won/lost.

## Contact-list fields
Every public-business prospect should capture:
- business name
- market
- service category
- public website
- public business email
- public business phone
- source URL
- relevant license/public credential source when applicable
- service territory
- rating/review signal
- last outreach
- status
- opt-out/suppression

Do not collect or use private/personal contact data that is not intended as a business contact channel.

## Outreach status
new → researched → ready → sent_1 → sent_2 → sent_3 → replied → partner/pilot → suppressed

## Automation boundary
Safe to automate:
- discovery queue creation
- enrichment from public business sources
- deduplication
- service/market classification
- draft personalization
- sending approved B2B sequences
- follow-up timing
- reply classification
- suppression/opt-out
- partner intake
- lead routing

Human/exception review initially:
- licensing ambiguity
- customer/vendor disputes
- refunds
- high-dollar exclusive contracts
- deceptive/uncertain business identity
- sensitive categories
