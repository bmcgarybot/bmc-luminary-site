# BMC Outreach Playbook

## Objective
Build supply and demand in parallel without requiring cold calls from Brett.

## Campaign A — Vendor recruitment
Target: local service providers that can fulfill BMC-generated leads.

### Sequence
1. Intro email
   - personalized to market + service
   - explain BMC is building a local lead/referral channel
   - invite provider to complete the partner intake
   - no fake claim that a lead is currently waiting
2. Follow-up 1 (3–4 business days)
   - short reminder
   - emphasize territory/service fit and no sales meeting required
3. Follow-up 2 (7–10 business days)
   - offer pilot placement / first qualified lead test where appropriate
4. Close loop
   - mark no-response and suppress for 60–90 days

### Credibility-leverage personalization
When a public source verifies that the company has already invested in a local credibility channel, use that as the personalized opener.

Examples:
- Chamber of Commerce membership
- BBB accreditation
- Firewise / defensible-space contractor lists
- county contractor lists
- trade-association membership
- local tourism or business-directory membership

Positioning:
"You already invested in local credibility. Let's help turn it into qualified work."

Important:
- Chamber membership and BBB accreditation are not interchangeable terms.
- Never claim a membership/accreditation unless verified from a public source.
- Treat these signals as personalization and quality signals, not proof that BMC endorses or guarantees the provider.

## Campaign B — B2B buyer acquisition
Target recurring and project buyers, including:
- restaurants and hospitality
- property managers, HOAs, apartments, STR managers
- fleets and auto businesses
- offices, retail, medical/dental
- **churches and religious campuses**
- **private schools, public-school facilities departments, charter schools**
- **theaters, performing-arts centers, cinemas, arenas and event venues**
- community centers, gyms and recreation facilities
- senior living and care facilities
- warehouses and light industrial

### Facility-buyer strategy
Churches, schools, theaters and venues are treated as **multi-service accounts**, not cleaning-only prospects.

Potential cross-sell categories:
- recurring janitorial
- one-time deep cleaning
- carpet / upholstery
- floor stripping, waxing, polishing and gym floors
- windows
- pressure washing
- event / post-event cleanup
- landscaping / grounds
- snow service where relevant
- junk / cleanouts
- parking lot sweeping / striping
- HVAC
- pest control
- fire-safety / suppression coordination
- plumbing / backflow
- signage / lighting

Outreach should lead with a real likely need, not dump a giant menu into the first email.

### Public-school rule
Public school districts often use procurement processes, existing facility contracts, or third-party facilities operators. Research the facilities/operations/procurement contact and contract structure first. Do not target teachers or classroom staff as service buyers.

### Sequence
1. Identify a concrete service fit.
2. Send a short plain-text email offering the request/quote route.
3. Link directly to the relevant service intake.
4. Follow up once or twice, then suppress.
5. For larger multi-service facilities, move positive replies into a facility-account / recurring-maintenance opportunity.

## Campaign C — Existing-lead vendor routing
Only after a real customer request exists.

1. Match exact market + service.
2. Send qualified job summary to active vendors.
3. Provider accepts/declines.
4. Route to next eligible vendor if declined/expired.
5. Release customer details according to the approved lead model.
6. Track accepted → contacted → estimate → won/lost.

## Contact-list fields
Every public-business prospect should capture:
- business name
- market
- service category
- public website
- public business email
- public business phone
- source URL
- relevant license/public credential source when applicable
- chamber / BBB / association signal
- service territory
- rating/review signal
- last outreach
- status
- opt-out/suppression

Do not collect or use private/personal contact data that is not intended as a business contact channel.

## Outreach status
new → researched → ready → sent_1 → sent_2 → sent_3 → replied → partner/pilot → suppressed

## Automation boundary
Safe to automate:
- discovery queue creation
- enrichment from public business sources
- deduplication
- service/market classification
- verified credibility-signal detection
- draft personalization
- sending approved B2B sequences
- follow-up timing
- reply classification
- suppression/opt-out
- partner intake
- lead routing

Human/exception review initially:
- licensing ambiguity
- public-sector procurement ambiguity
- customer/vendor disputes
- refunds
- high-dollar exclusive contracts
- deceptive/uncertain business identity
- sensitive categories
