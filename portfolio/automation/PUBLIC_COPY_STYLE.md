# BMC Public Copy Rules

Updated: 2026-10-25

## Core rule

**Customer pages explain what the customer can get or do. Internal documents explain how BMC works.**

Do not put strategy-conversation language, internal architecture, database concepts, routing logic, or business-model explanations on public customer pages.

## Reading level

Default to plain US English that a broad general audience can read quickly.

Target:
- roughly 6th-8th grade reading level;
- short sentences;
- common words;
- one idea per sentence;
- concrete verbs;
- customer language instead of operator language.

When a simpler common word works, use it.

Prefer:
- help
- fix
- clean
- remove
- repair
- quotes
- prices
- find
- compare
- building
- business
- customer
- job

Avoid on customer pages unless truly necessary:
- procurement
- taxonomy
- fulfillment
- routing
- service path
- classification
- institutional
- optimization
- monetization
- qualified prospect
- productized
- concierge
- coordination workflow
- backend

## Do not narrate the system to the customer

Bad:
- "You do not need to know how BMC categorizes it."
- "Search in normal words."
- "BMC routes your request."
- "You should not have to understand our backend."
- "Choose the outcome path."
- "The work behind the answer is the product."

Better:
- "What do you need help with?"
- "Search services."
- "Tell us what you need done."
- "Get quotes."
- "Find local help."
- "Need help keeping a building maintained?"
- "Need a better list of businesses to contact?"

The better versions do not explain BMC's internal thinking. They simply make the next action clear.

## Service names

Public names should sound like terms a customer would use.

Prefer:
- Electrician
- Heating & Air
- Handyman
- House Cleaning
- Tree Service
- Garage Door Repair
- Building Maintenance Help
- Restaurant Maintenance Help
- Get Business Quotes
- Get Equipment Quotes
- Cut Vendor Costs
- Check Local Competitors
- Research a New Market
- Find New Customers

Avoid public-facing names like:
- Institutional Facility Maintenance Concierge
- B2B Quote Sourcing Concierge
- Commercial Equipment Quote Concierge
- Local Competitor Intelligence Pack
- Local Market Expansion Pack

Internal service keys and technical labels may remain unchanged.

## Page structure

Customer pages should usually answer these questions in this order:

1. What can you help me with?
2. Is this for my kind of problem?
3. What do I do next?
4. What information do you need from me?
5. What happens after I submit?

Do not add an explanation just because BMC's internal system is complex.

## Buttons

Prefer:
- Get help
- Get quotes
- Start
- Send request
- See services
- Find help
- Check competitors
- Research this market
- Find customers

Avoid:
- Start workflow
- Submit intake
- Route request
- Explore service paths
- Activate concierge
- Begin procurement

## B2B rule

Business buyers can handle more detail, but the language should still sound normal.

"Compare three vendor quotes" is better than "multi-vendor procurement sourcing."

"Review regular service bills" is better than "vendor consolidation audit."

"100 businesses to contact" is better than "100 qualified prospect entities."

## Generation rule

Any generator that creates public pages must use these rules.

Before merging or deploying new public UI:
- check for internal/meta wording;
- check that service names sound customer-facing;
- read the hero copy out loud;
- if it sounds like a strategy memo, rewrite it.


## Do not sell our uncertainty

Public conversion copy must not foreground BMC's age, lack of case studies, limited launch geography, learning stage, or need to prove the model. Those facts can be true without becoming the headline.

Bad public framing:
- "BMC is new."
- "We have to prove this works."
- "We're starting only in Prescott and Prescott Valley."
- "Help us shape the pilot."
- "Try our experiment."

Better framing:
- "$0 to join."
- "See the request before you decide."
- "Set the jobs, area, minimum, and capacity that fit your business."
- "Pass when it is not a fit."
- "Any paid terms are shown before you agree."

Use campaign/outreach copy to localize the launch when useful. For example, provider recruitment may say "We're opening the network in Prescott," "We're opening in Prescott Valley," or "We're expanding across Northern Arizona" depending on the recipient. The generic website should present BMC as capable of serving the visitor, not as a geography-limited experiment.

Risk reversal should reduce the visitor's risk, not advertise BMC's risk. Necessary legal or expectation-setting language can remain in terms, FAQs, or application consent, but should not dominate the conversion story.
