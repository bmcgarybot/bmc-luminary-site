# BMC customer language and email standards

## Customer-facing search
- Always route actual customer descriptions to an active service category before showing a no-match.
- Curated phrases are deterministic and run before local AI. The local model handles unseen descriptions, not known examples.
- Specific equipment and repair terms beat generic words. Do not silently substitute home plumbing with specialist commercial backflow providers; until a dedicated general plumbing service is available, show an honest service label or capture an unclassified request and disclose availability.
- Query evaluation should include misspellings, plural forms, common symptoms, trades, materials, urgency, rural terms, abbreviations, and industry-specific expressions.
- Keep test fixtures for search phrases that users reported as failures. Do not delete historical cases to improve test pass rates.
- Never display a service as available unless there is a verified active provider for its market. Still let the customer ask for help with an honest availability message.
- Search interest is not a verified lead and must not be added to customer contact records without an actual submission and suitable consent.

## Human email writing brief for the VPS
Write like a helpful local business employee. Use clear sixth-to-eighth grade language. Short natural sentences, a real reason for contacting someone, one useful detail from verified data, and one straightforward next step. Avoid hype and generic filler. Never claim that researched prospects requested service or were waiting for the contractor. Never claim delivery when SMTP only accepted submission. No fake scarcity, invented results, or made-up familiarity.

### Prohibited stylistic patterns
- No em dashes (U+2014) or en dashes (U+2013) anywhere in Subject, plain-text body, or HTML.
- No phrases like: unlock your potential; game changer; revolutionary; in today's fast-paced world; leverage synergies; seamless solution; elevate your business; supercharge growth; excited to announce; just checking in; I hope this email finds you well.
- No aggressive multi-CTA formatting, unsupported guarantees, or giant promotional cards.

### Preferred first-touch structure
Subject: [City]: 5 commercial properties worth a look

Hi [first name],

I found five commercial properties in [city] that may be worth contacting for [actual service]. They fit [one specific, verified reason], although I cannot say they are actively hiring.

Want to see the five? [signed link]

If they fit the kind of work you want, we also offer a list of 100 qualified local prospects for $499.

[real sender and business information]
[postal address]
Reply no if you do not want more emails.

Only add claims supported by actual available inventory. One-click links must use the real signed path and the exact deployed owner preview before any sending. The human sender review gate is mandatory, not replaced by this example.

### Technical enforcement
The common send_mail function normalizes prohibited dash characters at the MIME creation boundary for all messages, including those generated by local AI. Keep tests for all delivery paths and never activate outbound to test a copy edit.

## Current scope
The catalog vocabulary library and CI regressions are committed on the isolated safety branch, not on the public VPS. This is a first coverage expansion, not proof that every search phrase is handled. A full 21-market search and verified-provider availability audit remains necessary.
