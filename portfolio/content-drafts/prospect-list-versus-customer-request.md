# A prospect list and a customer asking for a job are different products

A researched prospect is a business that appears to fit the kind of customer you want. A customer request starts with someone saying they need a specific job done. Both can help a service business, but they shouldn't be sold under the same promise.

If you run a pressure-washing or cleaning business, knowing the difference helps you judge what you're buying and what work comes next.

## What a researched prospect list gives you

A useful commercial prospect list should explain why each business fits your target, the market it belongs to, the source used and a usable public business contact route. It gives you a starting point for relevant outreach.

It does not prove that the business is currently shopping, has a budget, wants a quote or will respond. A high fit score describes the research criteria; it isn't a buying-intent score or a prediction that you'll win an account.

## What a confirmed customer request should establish

Before treating an incoming job request as a potential paid introduction, establish the service, area, scope, timing, contact route and customer's permission for the introduction. Check for duplicates and whether the job is still wanted.

A public “looking for help” post can be a demand signal. It isn't automatically a verified, consented introduction that can be resold. Similarly, a business advertising “we do pressure washing” is a potential provider prospect, not a homeowner asking to hire someone.

## How to evaluate the offer

For a research list, inspect the actual sample. Are the businesses in your area and suited to your service? Do the source and contact route make sense? Is the reason for fit useful enough to shape a relevant first approach?

For a customer introduction, ask what was confirmed, when it was confirmed, who receives the request and when any fee becomes payable. Read the terms for invalid details, duplicate charges, service/area mismatch and cancellation. A valid introduction still isn't a guaranteed job.

For either product, track what happens after delivery: relevant replies, conversations, estimates, won work, refunds and the time spent following up. Paying for a list or introduction is a business expense; it doesn't by itself demonstrate a return.

## A practical first message

Use a message that fits what you actually know. If you have only identified a possible commercial customer, don't say that it requested your help. Explain the service you provide, why it may be relevant to that property and a low-pressure next step. Respect the business's contact preferences and stop when it opts out.

If someone has confirmed a job request and agreed to an introduction, follow up about that request promptly and specifically. Use the contact information for that job, not for unrelated marketing or resale.
